Newhall Terrace Apartments is a 66-unit apartment building at 24377 Newhall Avenue in Santa Clarita, built in 1972 on a 2.14 acre parcel and containing approximately 36,908 gross square feet across two stories.

The property is a single floor plan asset. All 66 units are one bedroom, one bath homes of approximately 550 square feet, which makes the rent roll unusually simple to underwrite and unusually simple to manage: one unit type, one turn cost, one market rent to solve for.

The 58 occupied units average $1,584 per month. The market rent carried in the analysis is $1,900, which sits just under the average of the one-bedroom comparables in this submarket. Across those occupied units that gap is roughly $220,000 of annual rent the property is not currently collecting, and no entitlement, construction, or repositioning is required to reach it.

Because Santa Clarita has no local rent stabilization ordinance, the gap closes on turnover rather than through a capped annual increase. Seven units were vacant at the rent roll date, which means a portion of that repositioning is available to a buyer immediately rather than over a holding period.

Location Overview
Walking Distance to Metrolink in Old Town Newhall

The property sits on Newhall Avenue at the edge of Old Town Newhall. Newhall Metrolink Station is three tenths of a mile away, a six minute walk, putting a tenant on a one seat rail ride to Burbank, Glendale, and Union Station without a car. The Oaks Plaza is two tenths of a mile from the door and the Newhall Crossings retail and residential development is three tenths of a mile.

William S. Hart Regional Park is a twelve minute walk. College of the Canyons is 4.8 miles out and Henry Mayo Newhall Hospital, one of the valley's largest employers, is 1.9 miles away.

This is the walkable end of the Santa Clarita Valley, which is uncommon in a market built overwhelmingly around the car. For a building of 550 square foot one bedrooms, a tenant base that can live here without a second vehicle is not a soft amenity, it is what supports the rent.

The recommendation is built on current income rather than on the pro forma. Closed arm's-length sales in Newhall run from $176,607 to $209,091 per unit, and the only closed trade at comparable scale, a 65-unit building half a mile up the same street, allocated at $182,692 per unit inside a four-property portfolio transaction.

On a per unit basis the recommendation sits at the upper end of that closed range, and on income it does not. The one closed sale in the set carrying a confirmed cap rate traded at 5.42%, and the escrow half a mile away is at 5.31%. The recommended value prices this property in line with both while its gross rent multiplier stays below every income-supported comparable in the analysis.

Two things move this number more than the comparables do. The first is the rent gap: 20% of upside on a building with no local rent control is what justifies pricing at the top of the per unit range rather than the middle. The second is how the vacancies are carried. Five vacant units, the manager unit and two units of pending status, eight of 66, sit in scheduled rent at the $1,900 asking rent rather than at an achieved rent, which is standard practice for marketed vacancies and is what the current capitalization rate is struck on. The sensitivity table prices both that case and the owner's higher reported repairs and management.

The property was originally developed with 1987 low-income housing tax credits. That allocation carried a fifteen-year compliance period and no extended-use covenant, since the thirty-year requirement arrived only for allocations after 1989, and the restrictions expired in 2002. Ownership confirms the property carries no rent restriction and no age restriction today. Third-party data services still display the historical allocation, which is a record of a 1987 program rather than a live covenant. It does explain the rent profile: rents were held down for fifteen years and were never reset to market afterward.

Stabilized at market rent with utility bill-back in place, net operating income reaches $998,029. That figure is a projection of what the property can earn, not what it earns today, and it is not the basis of the recommendation.
